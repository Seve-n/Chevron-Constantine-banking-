# Business Questions

These are the questions this project is designed to answer. They come directly from the business
problem described in [project-brief.md](project-brief.md): Chevron Constantine Banking does not know where or why
customers abandon digital journeys.

Each question will later be mapped to specific data, KPIs, and analysis in
[data-requirements.md](data-requirements.md).

## Overall performance

1. What is the overall completion rate across all digital journeys?
2. What is the overall abandonment rate, and how has it varied across the observed period?

## Where customers abandon

3. At which step of each journey do most customers abandon?
4. Which journey has the highest abandonment rate overall?
5. Is abandonment concentrated in a few critical steps, or spread evenly across the journey?

## Why customers abandon (contributing factors)

6. Which steps generate the most errors, and are high-error steps also high-abandonment steps?
7. Does the time spent on a step relate to the likelihood of abandonment on that step?
8. Do customers who contact support still complete their journey, or do they abandon anyway?

## Who is affected

9. Are some customer segments (e.g., new vs. existing customers) more likely to abandon than
   others?
10. Are some age groups more affected than others?
11. Does the device type (mobile vs. desktop vs. tablet) affect completion rates?
12. Does the channel used (app, website, branch-assisted digital) affect completion rates?

## So what?

13. Which journey and which step should Chevron Constantine Banking prioritize fixing first, based on volume of
    customers affected?
14. What concrete product or process changes could reduce abandonment at the most problematic
    steps?
15. Which KPIs should Chevron Constantine Banking monitor going forward to know if those changes are working?

## A note on causality

Several of these questions ask "why" — but the dataset used in this project is observational,
not experimental. Findings will be phrased carefully (e.g., "higher error rates are associated
with higher abandonment on this step") rather than asserting a direct cause, unless the evidence
is strong enough to justify it. This distinction matters and is something I should be able to
explain clearly in an interview.
